A friend of mine has run several biopharma companies. He told me recently that getting at good data, even public data, is a nightmare. He said he would happily pay a premium just for someone to find the good stuff and put it in one place.

I hear a version of that from AI teams all the time. You build a model, and at some point you need data it has never seen to find out if it actually works. Public benchmarks are a good start but they run out fast. A lot of them are already in the training data, and none of them look like your customers' real problems.

So here is where evaluation data comes from today, what I would check before paying for it, and what to do when you can't find it.

## What teams are looking for

Most of the searches I see fall into a few buckets:

- Held-out test sets in a specific field: chemistry, materials, biology, code, finance.
- Real measurements a model has to predict: lab results, instrument data, sensor and process data.
- Expert-graded examples with rubrics, so you can score answers and not just compare text.
- Data from outside your own lab or company, to see if the model holds up somewhere new.

## Where it comes from today

- **Data and annotation vendors.** They build expert-graded sets to order. Good quality, and usually priced for enterprise budgets.
- **Data marketplaces.** Catalogues from many providers. Quick to browse, but specialist evaluation data is pretty thin.
- **Public repositories.** Hugging Face, Kaggle, government and research archives. Free, but scattered and uneven, and the licence often rules out commercial use.
- **The people who generated it.** Labs, test houses, instrument makers and research groups sitting on exactly the measurements you need. Nobody has ever asked them.

That last group is the biggest and the hardest to reach.

## What to check before you buy

- **Licence.** Evaluation only, training too, and is commercial use allowed?
- **Provenance.** Who generated it, when and how? Is the seller allowed to license it?
- **A sample.** Run it through your own tests before you commit.
- **Contamination.** Has it ever been published or scraped? If so, your model may have already seen it.
- **Fit.** Does it measure what your users care about, or just something close?
